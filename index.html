Mission 01 · 2025 – present

Clarity

A cognitive accessibility app for iOS that changes how dense information is presented to fit each person.

Watch pitch on YouTube ↗
6-min pitch · jump to

  1. Problem

    Dense information like articles, forms, and instructions comes in one format for everyone. For people who process information differently, that format can stop them from getting what they need.

  2. What I built

    An iOS app that adapts how dense information is presented to each user's cognitive needs. It combines on-device OCR, speech, and AI, so nothing leaves the phone. That keeps it private.

  3. My role

    Founder & solo developer, 8 hours a week. I did the design, the code, and the pitch.

  4. Tech

    • Swift
    • SwiftUI
    • On-device OCR
    • Speech
    • On-device AI
    • Xcode

  5. Result

    400+

    entrants in the Kode With Klossy × Apple App Challenge, where I entered Clarity.

  6. Learned / next

    Getting Clarity to work proved it was possible, not that anyone would use it. Pitching it taught me the hardest questions were who pays and how it reaches people who struggle with technology, which is why I’m designing a dedicated hardware device next.

Mission 04 · 2025 – 2026 · TSA Engineering Design

Mars Rover Dust Mitigation

A Wimshurst-inspired electrostatic generator to keep Martian dust out of rover wheel axles. Competition theme: "Tools of Scientific Discovery."

sketch → v1 → final · tap to enlarge

  1. Problem

    Martian dust gets into rover wheel axles, where it wears down moving parts on missions that can't be repaired.

  2. What I built

    A Wimshurst-inspired electrostatic generator with counter-rotating disks carrying aluminum sectors. A VEX motor drives them through 3D-printed pulleys and a figure-eight belt.

  3. My role

    Team leader & prototype engineer: I led the design and built the prototype.

  4. Tech

    • Electrostatics
    • Wimshurst machine
    • VEX motor
    • 3D printing
    • Belt drive

  5. Result

    3 fixes

    to spacing, collectors, and isolation turned a low-charge first build into a prototype that generated an electric field. That showed the charge-generation mechanism works.

  6. Learned / next

    A machine that spins isn’t the same as one that works. The failed first build taught me to test each subsystem’s output early instead of only at the end. Next, I’d measure field strength and how much dust it actually repels.

v1 · failed

The first prototype spun, but it produced too little charge to be useful.

→
diagnosis

We tested each part separately and found charge was leaking through the frame, and the collectors sat too far from the disks to pick up charge. That pointed us to spacing, collector placement, and isolation.

→
final · what changed

Adjusted disk spacing, moved the collectors, and improved electrical isolation. The final prototype generated an electric field.
